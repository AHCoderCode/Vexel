STUDY SKILLS

Learn with AI without outsourcing your thinking

An instant explanation can feel like learning, but recognition is not the same as being able to recall or apply an idea yourself. Use AI to create practice, give feedback, and expose gaps—not simply to do the difficult part for you.

A practical guide · About 6 minutes

A helpful AI tutor can rephrase a concept, create examples, or respond to a specific misunderstanding. It can also be confidently wrong, skip a step you need, or give away an answer before you have had a chance to think. A strong study routine keeps your own attempt at the centre.

Use the attempt–feedback–retry cycle

  1. Make an attempt. Write what you think the answer is or try the exercise before asking for a solution.
  2. Request a hint or diagnosis. Ask for one clue, a question that helps you reason, or feedback on a specific step.
  3. Correct your reasoning. Explain why the original attempt was wrong, not just which answer is right.
  4. Try a new example. Ask for a similar problem with different values or wording. Solve it without looking at the worked answer.
  5. Summarise from memory. Close the explanation and write the main idea in your own words. Then compare your version with a reliable course source or textbook.

This cycle gives you evidence about what you can actually do. If you only read a finished solution, you may understand it in the moment without being able to reproduce the method later.

Prompts that make AI behave more like a tutor

For a difficult concept

Explain [topic] at my current level.
Start with the idea in plain language.
Show one worked example and explain each step.
Then give me a new question one at a time.
Wait for my attempt before giving the answer.
If I am wrong, point out the first step to reconsider.

For checking your working

Here is my attempt: [your work].
Do not replace it with a complete solution yet.
Find the first step that is incorrect or unsupported.
Explain why it is a problem, then give me one hint
so I can try the correction myself.

For exam revision

Build a revision quiz from these topics: [topics].
Ask one multiple-choice question at a time.
After my answer, explain the reasoning and why
the other options do not fit. Track the topics I miss
and give me a short review set at the end.

Use the assistant to practise, not to replace your teacher’s syllabus, the required textbook, or the exam board’s official specification. Check key definitions and methods against the materials your course actually uses.

Turn mistakes into information

When you miss a question, label the reason. Did you forget a fact, misunderstand a word, choose the wrong formula, skip a step, or apply a familiar rule in the wrong situation? Different mistakes need different responses. Memorising more facts will not solve a problem caused by misunderstanding the question.

You can ask an AI tutor to help categorise your error, but judge the diagnosis against your actual working. If it assumes a step that you did not write or misreads your answer, correct that context before continuing.

Check that you can work without assistance

After studying, put away the chat and try a fresh problem. Explain the concept aloud or write the steps from memory. If you are stuck, identify the missing step and return to the source material. Revisit the same idea later rather than repeating the explanation immediately until it feels familiar.

  • Can I explain it? Describe the idea in language you would use with a classmate.
  • Can I apply it? Solve a new problem, not just the example you already saw.
  • Can I spot a wrong answer? Explain why a plausible alternative fails.
  • Can I remember it later? Test yourself again after a gap.

Use AI fairly and responsibly

Follow your school or course rules about AI assistance. Do not present generated work as your own when the assignment requires independent work. If AI use is allowed, use it to understand the material, brainstorm, or receive feedback, and disclose that use when your teacher’s rules require it.

Do not upload classmates’ private work, personal records, passwords, or other information you do not have permission to share. If an answer seems to contradict your course materials, ask your teacher or consult the assigned source instead of assuming the chatbot is correct.

The goal

The best study session leaves you more capable without the tool. Measure success by what you can explain and solve independently, not by how quickly the assistant can produce a polished answer.
